5. Draw what the observer sees

The eye view is drawn with nothing but scalar products, vector products and division. Work out where a corner of a cuboid appears on the screen, then check it. Verified corners appear as pink dots in the observer's eye view.

Step A. The right-hand direction rStep A. The camera axes r and u

The observer stands at P and the eye is 1.5 above it: E = (Px, Py, 1.5). The facing direction is your f from section 1 with z-component 0, f = (a, b, 0), and the sky is k = (0, 0, 1).

Extension: f = (a, b, c), where c says how far the observer looks up (+) or down (−). Find both r = f × k and u = r × f.

f, r and k all have length 1, so each scalar product is simply “how many steps in that direction”. Why divide by the depth? Similar triangles: imagine a glass window one step in front of the eye. A point 7 ahead and 1 to the right crosses the window at 1 ÷ 7, so further objects look smaller.

Extension: what if the observer looks up or down?

Giving f a z-component c makes the observer look up (c > 0) or down (c < 0). The top of the screen is then no longer the sky k but u = r × f, which tilts with the camera. f, r and u may not have length 1, so turn them into unit vectors first.

Unit vectorsf̂ = f ÷ |f|, r̂ = r ÷ |r|, û = u ÷ |u|
Depthv · f̂

Upv · û
Screen x, screen yacross ÷ depth, up ÷ depth

Questions to explore

  • If c > 0, which way does u tilt away from k? Predict before you calculate.
  • When the observer looks up, does the horizon move up or down the screen? Check in the eye view.
  • Why do these formulas become the basic ones when c = 0 and |f| = 1?
  • If |f| ≠ 1 and you do not use unit vectors, how are the screen coordinates wrong?
  • Why can r not be found when the observer looks straight up, f = (0, 0, 1)?
